# Wall movement and faction combat update

2026-09-08 user-requested behavior revision. This supersedes the earlier nonlethal-only encounter concept for the implemented game. Geography, faction behavior and encounters are fictional arcade rules, not factual depictions of real incidents.

- **Collision:** Alex and pursuers use radius-0.22 circles against every potentially overlapping solid tile AABB. Movement is split into segments no longer than 0.1 tile (or half the actor radius) and resolves X/Y separately for sliding. This prevents long-step tunneling through adjoining walls and diagonal corner penetration. Gate closure checks whole actor radii; dead bodies are nonblocking.
- **Perception:** A 70-degree cone follows each pursuer's actual facing, with configured range and wall visibility. Candidates are Alex and living hostile faction members. Nearest visible candidate wins deterministically. Even partial suspicion moves toward the last visible position. At a full meter the pursuer commits and can fire. Hidden targets do not update their remembered position: suspicion drains over 1.2 seconds, then the actor clears the target and returns to patrol. The stop-at-zero rule also ends full-meter pursuit; there is no omniscient chase through cover.
- **Faction matrix:** Same faction never fights itself. ICE and Border Patrol are allied and do not fire on one another. Cartel and paramilitary actors are hostile to each other and to either federal faction. All pursuers can detect/chase Alex. The optional civilian companion is not a gunfire target; helping them remains a safe-shelter action. These explicit game rules avoid accidental friendly fire or an implication that every distinct uniform is automatically hostile.
- **Combat:** Full-meter actors with line of sight within 3.6 tiles fire every 0.8 seconds. Faction members have 100 health and take 25 damage per hit. Alex has 100 health and takes 20; reaching zero resets the district and restores health/resources. Spawn grace suppresses player damage. Shots resolve after all actors decide their actions, allowing simultaneous return fire. A surviving victim identifies its attacker and can retaliate. Shots cannot pass through walls.
- **Distractions:** Hostile investigators arriving at the same beacon deliberately target one another and start a shootout. Same-faction and allied investigators argue for two seconds, then recover; they never damage one another. The earlier fixed eight-second harmless rival dust-cloud window is replaced by health-based combat. No points are awarded for injury or deaths.
- **Death and presentation:** Zero-health faction members stay prone at their final location until district reset. They do not move, detect, shoot, capture, attract new targets or block paths. There is no gore. Health/suspicion bars distinguish injury from awareness. Brief localized muzzle flashes/tracer cues are disabled by reduced motion; gameplay timing and damage remain identical.
- **Walking:** Gait phase advances from actual collision-resolved distance, not elapsed time. Stationary or fully blocked characters do not walk in place. Reduced motion removes cosmetic limb bobbing.

The existing three-district walking campaign fixture remains unchanged and passes with the live updated pursuers present. Regression cases cover wall tunneling, adjoining corners, sliding, partial suspicion, last-seen behavior, autonomous rival acquisition, damage/death persistence, allies, dead nonblocking actors, player-health checkpoint recovery and displacement-driven walking.

Vision wedges use the same closed-tile intersection as detection, stop at cover and map edges, and follow the observer's heading. Distracted investigators do not show a detection wedge while they ignore perception. The mint dashed aiming boundary shows the beacon placement radius; a red cross marks an invalid target. Characters use front/back/side views aligned with projected world headings.

Sprint has four seconds of capacity in every mode, displayed above the playfield. Moving at sprint speed drains 25 stamina per second; walking/resting restores 25 per second. Exhaustion requires releasing sprint before another burst, preventing automatic speed flicker while the key stays held. Water remains available to collect after stopping a sprint. Pause freezes stamina; checkpoint/district resets refill it.
